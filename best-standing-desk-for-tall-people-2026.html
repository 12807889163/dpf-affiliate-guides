How to pick a standing desk that fits a tall body

Three things actually matter for users above 6'2\":

1. Max height, not min. Most sit-stand desks range from about 28 to 48 inches. If you're 6'4\" or taller you need at least 49 inches of max height, or your monitor ends up below your line of sight at standing position. The Uplift V3 (50.9\"), Vari Ergo (50.5\"), and FlexiSpot E7 Pro 71-inch (48.4\") are the only ones on this list that hit ergonomic height for users above 6'4\".

2. Dual-motor stability past 60 inches. Single-motor frames wobble when you lean on them at full standing height, and the wobble gets worse on a 71- or 72-inch top. Every pick on this list is dual-motor for a reason. If you see a single-motor desk in the 71+ inch range, it's a red flag.

3. Top depth, not just width. Width matters for monitor arms, but depth matters for forearm clearance. A 6'5\" user with elbows out at standing position needs at least 28 inches of top depth, ideally 30. The BANTI 79x32 has the most depth in this test group, and the Uplift V3 72x30 is a close second.

FAQ

Q: What is the right max height for someone 6'4"?

A: At least 49 inches for ergonomic standing posture, with elbows at a 90-degree angle and the monitor top at eye level. The Uplift V3 (50.9\"), Vari Ergo (50.5\"), and most 49+ inch frames in this list will fit. For users 6'6\" to 6'9\" the Uplift V3 with the 72-inch top is the safest pick because the deeper top lets you push the monitor back without crowding the keyboard.

Q: Is a single-motor standing desk strong enough for a tall user?

A: Not really. Single-motor frames work for users up to about 6'0\" who don't lean on the desk at standing height. Past 6'2\" you need dual-motor stability because the longer lever arm from a taller user creates wobble that a single motor can't dampen. Every pick on this list is dual-motor for that reason.

Q: How wide should the top be for a dual-monitor setup at 6'5"?

A: At least 71 inches. A 6'5\" user with a 49-inch ultrawide plus a vertical 27-inch second monitor needs about 60 inches of width just for the monitors, plus 6 to 8 inches of clearance on each side. The BANTI 79-inch and the Fully Jarvis 72-inch are the two widest on this list.
