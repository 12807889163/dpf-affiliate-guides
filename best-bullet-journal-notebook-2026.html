How to choose the right bullet journal notebook in 2026

The big three: pen, paper weight, and lay-flat binding. Everything else is decoration.

1. Match the paper to your pen. This is the single biggest decision and most people get it backwards. They pick a notebook because the cover is pretty, then discover the paper ghosts with their pen of choice. If you use a fountain pen, brush pen, or watercolor, you need 160gsm. If you use a fine-liner or ballpoint, 80-100gsm is fine and the book will be lighter and cheaper.

2. Lay-flat binding matters more than you think. A notebook that fights you when you open it to a mid-spread will slowly kill the habit. Test the binding in person if you can. If you are buying online, the Leuchtturm and BUKE lay flat out of the box. The Moleskine and some of the cheaper RETTACY copies need to be cracked first.

3. Page numbering saves setup time. Numbered pages and a printed index are the entire reason the official Bullet Journal method works. If you have to number 200 pages by hand before you can use the book, you will skip the index. Skipping the index means you will not be able to find anything two months in. The numbered-page feature is worth the few extra dollars.

4. Size is a real choice, not a default. A5 is the standard and it is the right size for most people. But if you do detailed daily spreads, try B5. If you do weekly-only spreads, try B6 or A6. The Archer & Olive size range is the main reason to pay their premium.

5. Page count affects how often you finish a book. A 192-page book lasts about 4-5 months at one page per day. A 320-page book lasts 7-8 months. If you do not like finishing books, get a smaller page count so the "new notebook" dopamine hit comes more often.

6. Cover design is real, not vanity. A cover you are embarrassed to take out in a coffee shop will end up in your bag. Pick a design you actually like.

7. Return policy is the safety net. Buy from a seller with a 30-day return window. If the paper ghosts with your pen, return it and try the next one on the list. The price difference between these notebooks is small enough that the trial-and-error cost is low.

FAQ

Q: Is the LEUCHTTURM1917 Bullet Journal Edition 2 worth the premium over the RETTACY?

A: Only if you will use the numbered pages and index. The official method depends on those features. If you are going to ignore the index and just write daily pages, save the $15 and buy the RETTACY. The paper is thinner on the Leuchtturm and the cover design is identical from a distance.

Q: What is the best notebook for fountain pen users?

A: Scribbles That Matter or Archer & Olive, both at 160gsm. The Leuchtturm 80gsm paper will show through with most fountain pen inks, especially wet ones. If you are using a Pilot Metropolitan with a fine nib and a dry ink, the Leuchtturm is workable. For everything else, go 160gsm.

Q: I am left-handed. Does that change the recommendation?

A: Not on paper weight or brand. It does change which pen you use. Avoid fountain pens and brush pens if you are a lefty who smears. go with ballpoint or fast-drying gel pens like the Uni-ball Signo. With the right pen, any notebook on this list works for a lefty.

Q: How long does a 249-page bullet journal last?

A: Depends on how you use it. If you do one page per day (a daily log), about 8 months. If you do weekly spreads with collections, more like 10-12 months. If you do a daily page plus a collection page, 6-7 months. The official method is built around a year per book.

Q: Should I get A5 or B5?

A: A5 unless you have a specific reason for B5. A5 fits in most laptop bags and the spread sizes are well documented online. B5 is bigger and gives you more room for daily spreads, but it does not fit in a standard handbag.

Q: Do I really need the elastic closure and the back pocket?

A: The elastic keeps the book shut in a bag. The back pocket holds stickers, loose notes, and a spare pen. Neither is essential, but after using a notebook with both, going back feels like a downgrade.

Q: How do I avoid the "half-finished drawer" problem?

A: Different problem than the notebook, but two things help. First, do a monthly review on the last page of the month. Second, finish a book before you start a new one. The biggest predictor of an abandoned bullet journal is starting a new notebook before the old one is done.
